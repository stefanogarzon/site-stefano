CHAMPION-AF (Doshi et al., N Engl J Med 2026; DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2517213) randomised 3000 anticoagulation-eligible AF patients to left atrial appendage closure (LAAC) or a direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC). Closure was noninferior for the composite of cardiovascular death, stroke or systemic embolism at 3 years. The reading changes once the composite is broken down.

## Stroke alone in the three trials

| Trial | Stroke, LAAC vs DOAC | Estimate |
|---|---|---|
| CHAMPION-AF (3 years) | 3.6% vs 2.5% | HR 1.46 (95% CI 0.94 to 2.27) |
| CLOSURE-AF | 2.6 vs 2.7 per 100 patient-years | — |
| PRAGUE-17 (3.5 years) | Stroke or TIA | sHR 1.14 (0.56 to 2.30) |

In CHAMPION-AF, the stroke interval nearly reaches significance against the device. In CLOSURE-AF, considered a negative trial, stroke rates were the same. PRAGUE-17 is underpowered but shows no trend against the device. CHAMPION-AF, with 3000 patients, shows a numerical excess of stroke with LAAC.

## How CHAMPION-AF met noninferiority

- **Absolute margin set for a different event rate.** The 4.8 percentage-point margin was planned for a 12% event rate in each arm. The observed rate was about 5% (4.8% vs 5.7%). At that rate, the margin tolerates a relative risk well above 1.4.
- **Low-risk population.** A low event rate with a fixed absolute margin makes noninferiority easier.
- **A composite that dilutes stroke.** Cardiovascular death was 2.7% vs 2.7%. Systemic embolism was near zero (2 vs 0 events). Two components LAAC was never expected to improve dilute the one signal that matters, stroke.

## Why CLOSURE-AF failed

CLOSURE-AF missed noninferiority because of excess cardiovascular death and major bleeding. The excess came from procedural risk in an older, sicker cohort (CHA₂DS₂-VASc 5.2, HAS-BLED 3.0). On stroke prevention, it showed parity.

## Bleeding

The bleeding reduction in CHAMPION-AF (HR 0.55; 0.45 to 0.67) is real and clinically relevant. The primary safety endpoint, however, is non-procedure-related bleeding. In the secondary endpoint that adds procedural bleeding, the gap narrows: 5.9% vs 6.4%.

## Reading the three together

Across the three trials, LAAC consistently reduces non-procedural bleeding. LAAC does not consistently match DOAC on stroke prevention. Claiming noninferiority without breaking down the composite leaves the information incomplete.

Patients want to know their stroke risk. On that endpoint, current data do not allow us to say DOAC and LAAC are equivalent.
