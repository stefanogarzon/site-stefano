EuroIntervention. 2026;22(14):e773-e783."
doi: 10.4244/EIJ-D-26-00421
design: Open-label multicentre randomised pilot trial (the Netherlands; NCT05986968)
n: 200

draft: false
summary: |
  STOP-IMH randomised 200 STEMI patients to ticagrelor monotherapy or ticagrelor plus aspirin, directly after primary PCI. Follow-up was 13 months. A cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) substudy assessed intramyocardial haemorrhage (IMH).

  | Outcome | Ticagrelor (n = 98) | DAPT (n = 101) | HR (95% CI) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | MACCE | 4.1% | 4.0% | 1.04 (0.26 to 4.17) |
  | Clinically relevant bleeding | 4.1% | 10.9% | 0.37 (0.12 to 1.17) |
  | Non-access-site bleeding | 2.0% | 9.9% | 0.20 (0.04 to 0.92) |
strengths: |
  - Protocol published in advance, DSMB oversight and blinded event adjudication.
  - CMR substudy built to test the mechanism.
  - The authors state the study is a pilot and exploratory.
  - The bleeding reduction points the same way as TICO and TARGET-FIRST.
problems: |
  - **Uninformative MACCE interval.** HR 1.04 (0.26 to 4.17) with about 4 events per arm. The minimum detectable effect is around HR > 7. A 4-fold increase in ischaemic risk could go undetected. Concluding "no difference in ischaemic events" reads absence of evidence as evidence of absence.
  - **Fragile bleeding finding.** HR 0.20 (0.04 to 0.92) has a fragility index of 1. It is the only significant result among many secondary endpoints.
  - **No Kaplan-Meier curves or Schoenfeld test.** Cox HRs are reported without curves. Bleeding hazard clusters in months 1 to 3 and ischaemic hazard spreads across the year. Proportional hazards remain an untested assumption.
  - **CMR signal favouring DAPT.** In 51 patients, 7 of 8 structural readouts favour DAPT: infarct size (15.4% vs 9.9% of LV), IMH presence (42% vs 32%), microvascular obstruction presence (44% vs 35%), LVEF (47% vs 53%, p = 0.06), LV mass index (p = 0.04), LV end-systolic volume index and LV thrombus (22% vs 13%). The exception is microvascular obstruction size (3.6% vs 4.4% of LV), with heavily overlapping IQRs. By binomial test, 7 of 8 in the same direction gives p ≈ 3.5%.
  - **Incomplete explanation by the authors.** The authors attribute the signal to chance and to universal aspirin loading. VerifyNow shows 58% of the monotherapy arm still had full aspirin inhibition at days 2 to 4, when IMH expands. That would explain similar arms, not worse numbers in the monotherapy arm. Another reading: the design cannot test the hypothesis it was built for.
  - **Highly selected population.** Killip I in 98.5%, final TIMI 3 in 91.5%, no shock, no severe CKD, no oral anticoagulation, no recent MI. Five high-volume Dutch centres with 97% radial access.
opinion: |
  As a pilot, STOP-IMH does its job: it shows the design is feasible and raises the question of whether immediate aspirin omission holds up mechanistically. It does not inform practice.

  I disagree with the clinical translation in the abstract, discussion and "Impact on Daily Practice" section. Higher-risk patients, where the bleeding-ischaemia trade-off matters most, were not in the trial. The CMR signal deserves a more cautious read. To me, it points against monotherapy.

  NEO-MINDSET STEMI and STOP-DAPT 3 signalled trouble with early aspirin withdrawal, and STOP-IMH points the same way. COMPARE STEMI ONE should answer the question. Until then, the reading the methods section gives holds: a hypothesis-generating study.
