summary: |
  The meta-analysis included 13 observational studies with 15,941 patients (ViV-TMVR 5465; redo-SMVR 10,476).

  | Early outcome (ViV-TMVR vs redo-SMVR) | RR (95% CI) |
  |---|---|
  | In-hospital mortality | 0.72 (0.57 to 0.90) |
  | Stroke | 0.49 (0.29 to 0.83) |
  | Bleeding | 0.43 (0.20 to 0.94) |
  | Acute kidney injury | 0.57 (0.42 to 0.77) |
  | Pacemaker | 0.30 (0.19 to 0.49) |

  Length of stay was 5 days shorter with ViV-TMVR. Thirty-day mortality did not differ.

  At 5 years, survival did not differ: HR 0.92 (0.81 to 1.05). The proportional hazards assumption was violated (Schoenfeld p = 0.002). The authors used a flexible parametric B-spline model and a 6-month landmark: HR 0.69 (0.58 to 0.83) before and HR 1.47 (1.20 to 1.79) after.
strengths: |
  - Meta-analysis done properly for the most part, searching four databases.
  - Coherent early results: a less invasive procedure with less acute morbidity.
  - Proportional hazards violation tested and handled with a flexible model.
  - The authors acknowledge the lack of adjustment and are cautious in their conclusions.
problems: |
  - **ViV-TMVR patients were frailer.** They were older, with more diabetes, COPD and atrial fibrillation, and a higher STS score in 5 of the 7 studies that reported it. In-hospital mortality favored ViV-TMVR, but 30-day mortality did not. This implies excess deaths between discharge and day 30 in the ViV-TMVR arm, as expected in frailer patients.
  - **Post hoc cutoff.** The 6-month landmark was chosen by visual inspection of the HR(t) curve generated by the same data. The reversal is real; the exact split is post hoc.
  - **No patient-level adjustment.** Individual data reconstructed from published curves carry no covariates, propensity matching or E-value. The late HR of 1.47 fits at least three causal models: lower ViV-TMVR durability (underexpansion, residual gradients, hypoattenuated leaflet thickening); shorter baseline life expectancy; or a mix of both.
  - **Low E-value.** For HR 1.47, the E-value at the point estimate is about 2.3. Residual risk, frailty index and severity of bioprosthetic failure can all clear that bar.
opinion: |
  The study shows that ViV-TMVR is a procedure with less acute morbidity, in the populations currently selected for it.

  The study does not show that redo surgery gives better late survival in comparable patients. The reversal at 6 months fits confounding by indication as well as prosthesis durability. Valve deterioration driving mortality as early as 6 months seems unlikely to me; confounding by indication explains it better.

  The authors are cautious in the text. To me, the problem is how the study will be cited: as proof that redo surgery offers better late durability.
