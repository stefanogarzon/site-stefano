title: 'PIONEER IV (final result): QFR in every lesion did not meet non-inferiority'
date: 2026-11-08
lang: en
description: In the intention-to-treat analysis, QFR-guided PCI of every lesion was not non-inferior to usual care, in which 45.7% of patients had physiological assessment.
reference: PIONEER IV, 1-year result. Presented at TCT 2026. SOLACI summary, 5 Nov 2026. Interim analysis published in Eur Heart J. 2026.
doi: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehag174
design: Randomized non-inferiority trial, 27 European centers, all-comers population
'n': 2130
summary: |-
  PIONEER IV randomized 2,130 all-comers at 27 European centers to QFR-guided PCI (n=1,063) or usual care (n=1,067). In the QFR arm, a lesion was stented if pre-PCI QFR was ≤0.80. After stenting, the result was optimized if QFR was <0.91 or the delta >0.05. In usual care, the operator decided, with FFR, iFR, IVUS or OCT allowed. Both arms received the same stent (HT Supreme, SINOMED).

  The primary endpoint was POCE at 1 year: death, stroke, MI or revascularization. The non-inferiority margin was 3.2 percentage points in absolute difference.

  There is no paper on the final result yet. The numbers come from the TCT 2026 presentation and the [SOLACI summary](https://solaci.org/en/2026/11/05/tct-2026-pioneer-iv-angio-ffr-for-lesion-selection-and-optimization-in-percutaneous-coronary-intervention/). The DOI above belongs to the interim analysis, which has [its own review](/en/reviews/pioneer-iv-qfr/).

  | 1-year outcome | QFR | Usual care | Result |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | POCE, intention to treat | 7.4% | 6.1% | Upper limit 3.4, above the margin. Non-inferiority not met |
  | POCE, per protocol | 5.5% | 5.3% | Non-inferior |
  | Spontaneous MI | 3.0% | 1.7% | Mostly in non-target vessels |

strengths: |-
  - Randomized, multicenter, all-comers population.
  - Same stent in both arms. The difference between arms lies in the decision strategy.
  - QFR thresholds defined before and after stenting.
  - Intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses reported side by side.
problems: |-
  - **Comparator.** In usual care, 45.7% of patients had physiological assessment. The other 54.3% were treated on angiography alone. Against that arm, QFR in every lesion, before and after stenting, was at best non-inferior. In FAVOR III China, against angiography alone, QFR had an HR of 0.65 ([Xu et al., Lancet 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(21)02248-0)).

  <figure><img src="/images/avaliacoes/pioneer-iv-tct-2026/cuidado-usual.en.svg" width="680" height="130" alt="In the usual-care arm, 45.7% had physiological assessment and 54.3% were treated on angiography alone." loading="lazy"><figcaption>Figure 3. Use of physiology in the usual-care arm.</figcaption></figure>

  - **The authors' explanation.** They attribute the ITT result to adherence: 15.8% of the QFR arm deviated from protocol. POCE was 14.9% in non-adherent patients and 5.8% in adherent ones. That comparison is between nonrandomized groups. Non-adherent patients may be sicker or more complex, so the gap does not isolate the effect of QFR. The per-protocol analysis has the same limit.
  - **The MI signal.** The excess in the QFR arm came from spontaneous MI, 3.0% vs 1.7%, mostly in non-target vessels. That excess is hard to attribute to the PCI strategy and may be chance.
  - **Difference without a full interval.** The 1.3-point difference is my calculation (7.4 − 6.1). The lower limit of the interval was not reported.
  - **Endpoint definition.** The SOLACI summary describes the POCE revascularization as "clinically or physiologically driven". The exact definition will have to come from the paper.
opinion: |-
  Three data points soften the reading against QFR. In usual care, operators measure the intermediate lesion and decide the obvious ones on angiography, so the 45.7% may cover almost every lesion where physiology changes management. Deferral rates were similar in both arms, 35.3% vs 33.1%. The population had low complexity, with a SYNTAX score of 9.7 and 31.8% acute coronary syndrome, and the benefit of physiology tends to be smaller in that setting.

  In FAVOR III Europe, against FFR, QFR also missed non-inferiority, with an HR of 1.63 ([Andersen et al., Lancet 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(24)02175-5)). Taken together with that trial, PIONEER IV does not support QFR as a substitute for the wire where the wire is available.

  One number is missing to close the question: POCE in usual care with and without physiology.

  Do you use QFR to decide which lesions to treat, or do you still prefer the wire?
