title: 'PORTICO IDE at 5 years: death or stroke, attrition and the word "similar"'
date: 2026-10-24
lang: en
description: Death or stroke at 5 years was not significantly different, with an interval that includes 12 more events per 100 patients with Portico. Similarity was not tested.
reference: Makkar RR, Cheng W, Waksman R, Chakravarty T, Satler LF, Groh M, et al. Five-Year Follow-Up of Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement With Self-Expanding Intra-Annular Versus Commercially Available Valves. JACC Cardiovasc Interv. 2026 (published online).
doi: 10.1016/j.jcin.2026.05.032
design: Open-label randomized clinical trial, 5-year follow-up (NCT02000115)
'n': 750
summary: |-
  Between 2014 and 2017, PORTICO IDE randomized 750 high or extreme surgical risk patients to the self-expanding intra-annular Portico valve (n=381) or to commercially available TAVR valves (n=369). The authors conclude that Portico had "similar 5-year overall survival, stroke rates, and valve function". I read the paper and the supplement. I did not read the protocol or the SAP, so everything below refers to the published material.

  | 5-year outcome (ITT) | Portico | Commercial | p |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Death or stroke | 55.6% | 51.5% | 0.48 |
  | Cardiovascular death | 33.4% | 25.8% | 0.06 |
  | TAV reintervention | 3.4% | 0.9% | 0.031 |
  | Naive pacemaker | 36.6% | 20.3% | <0.0001 |
  | Mean gradient (mmHg) | 7.2 | 9.5 | 0.008 |
  | Valve area (cm²) | 1.78 | 1.71 | 0.40 |

  For death or stroke, the adjusted HR was 1.07 (95% CI 0.87 to 1.32).
strengths: |-
  - Randomized, with an active comparator of valves in clinical use.
  - Five-year clinical follow-up with Kaplan-Meier standard errors in Table 2, which allow the risk difference to be calculated.
  - Clinical outcomes reported by intention to treat and as treated.
problems: |-
  - **No test of similarity.** The paper gives no confidence interval for the difference. From the Kaplan-Meier standard errors in Table 2, I calculated +4.1 percentage points (−3.8 to +12.0). That interval fits no difference. It also fits 12 more patients per 100 dead or with stroke. No 5-year non-inferiority or equivalence margin is reported. A p-value of 0.48 means no difference was detected. Similarity needs its own margin and its own test.
  - **Endpoint changed.** The primary efficacy endpoint was death or disabling stroke at 1 year. At 5 years the headline is death or any stroke. I can't tell from what I read whether that was prespecified.
  - **Attrition.** Supplement S1 lists 147 consent withdrawals after randomization (19.6%), and 241 patients (32%) completed the 5-year visit. The split by arm is not given. 330 patients died (44%), so death explains a good part of the gap. Kaplan-Meier still assumes that the withdrawn patients carry the same risk as those who stayed.
  - **Echo on survivors who came to the visit.** The 5-year gradient rests on 74 and 76 patients, and valve area on 62 and 62.
  - **Cardiovascular death.** The as-treated p was 0.04. The Discussion links the excess to early deaths from vascular complications. By my calculation, the 30-day difference is 1.5 of the 7.6 points in ITT (20%) and 2.9 of 8.0 points as treated (36%). Most of the gap accrues later. Interval hazard ratios derived from the landmark curves are about 0.90 from day 30 to year 1 and 1.46 from year 1 to 5. They have no confidence intervals, so they suggest a late divergence without excluding an early excess. The Kaplan-Meier estimate censors non-CV deaths. Events after 2 years were assessed by sponsor safety personnel, and the paper does not say whether that assessment was masked.
  - **Valve function is mixed.** The gradient is lower with Portico. Moderate or greater paravalvular leak was 6.3% vs 2.1% at 30 days. At 5 years, mild or greater leak was 33.3% vs 12.5% (72 patients per arm). TAV reintervention and pacemaker rates were higher with Portico. The conclusion says "similar or superior".
  - **Stroke figures reversed.** The text and the Central Illustration give 5-year stroke as 13.2% for Portico and 11.6% for the commercial valves. Table 2, Figure 1C and Supplement S2 show the reverse.
opinion: |-
  What I think the paper establishes: in a first-generation device randomized in 2014 to 2017, death or stroke at 5 years was not significantly different, with an interval that includes harm, and with 32% of the cohort completing the 5-year visit. Pacemaker and TAV reintervention rates were higher with Portico.

  What it does not establish: similarity of death or stroke, early vascular complications as the cause of the CV death excess, or equivalent durability. The next-generation Navitor valve was not tested here.

  What margin would you accept for death or stroke at 5 years in this population?
