title: 'SUBLIME: the LIRA method for TAVR in bicuspid valves'
date: 2026-11-14
lang: en
description: LIRA was feasible in 6 centers, with 93% device success, but without a comparator the result cannot be attributed to the method.
reference: Bellini B, Romano V, Iannopollo G, Gorla R, Moccetti F, De Felice F, et al. Clinical Performance and Reproducibility of the LIRA Method for TAVR in Bicuspid Anatomy. The SUBLIME Study. JACC Cardiovasc Interv. 2026;19(18):2501-2511.
doi: 10.1016/j.jcin.2026.06.018
design: Retrospective multicenter single-arm cohort (6 European centers, Sep 2018 to Jul 2025; NCT06657911)
'n': 215
summary: |-
  SUBLIME included 215 patients with bicuspid valves (Sievers 1: 207; Sievers 2: 8) and severe aortic stenosis, treated with a self-expanding supra-annular valve. Size came from the LIRA method: the smaller perimeter between the raphe level and the annulus. 160 (74.4%) were downsized. Age 79.9 ± 6.1 years, STS 3.25%.

  | Outcome | Result |
  |---|---|
  | Device success, 30 days | 93.0% (200/215; 95% CI 88.8 to 95.7) |
  | Permanent pacemaker, 30 days | 10.7% (23/215; 7.2 to 15.5) |
  | Stroke, 30 days | 1.9% (4/215; 0.7 to 4.7) |
  | Moderate or greater paravalvular leak | 4.6% (10/215) |
  | Death at 1 year | 5.7% (8/140) |

  CIs are Wilson intervals, my calculation. For reproducibility, in 61 patients, κ 0.85 (0.74 to 0.96) and ICC 0.76 (0.63 to 0.85). The authors conclude the data confirm the method's consistency, safety and clinical applicability.
strengths: |-
  - A practical question: how to size the prosthesis in bicuspid valves, where the annulus misleads.
  - Six centers, showing the method travels beyond the center that created it.
  - An inter-reader reproducibility analysis.
problems: |-
  - **No comparator and no performance goal.** "Favorable" is judged against published series from other populations. The Discussion sets 30-day stroke (1.9%) beside the 1-year stroke of NOTION-2 (6.1%). With predilation in 94.8% and postdilation in 33.9%, the method's effect cannot be separated from the rest of the procedure.
  - **Selection and measurement.** The cohort spans 7 years without a flow diagram. The core lab and CT reads belong to the coordinating center, which published prior experience with the method, and no independent events committee is declared. Poorly sized patients or those without adequate CT may have been left out (unknown from the material read).
  - **One year in 65.1% of the cohort.** Table 5 uses N = 140 and crude proportions (death 8/140; CI 2.9 to 10.9). Patients enrolled up to Jul 2025 have less than 1 year of follow-up.
  - **Reproducibility in 61 patients.** The bias of −1.71 mm has a 95% CI of −3.16 to −0.26 mm (my calculation, assuming 61 pairs), which excludes zero; the text says "no systematic bias". Limits of −12.78 to +9.35 mm span about one prosthesis size. The rule for size agreement is not in the text.
  - **Between-center heterogeneity.** The mixed model has 6 centers and 15 device failures for 7 covariates, about 2 events per variable. The CI of the center variance runs from 0.16 to 10.27. "No heterogeneity" describes lack of precision.
  - **Conflicts.** The first author reports consulting for Medtronic, and other authors have ties with Medtronic, Abbott and Boston Scientific. Medtronic valves account for 180/215 (83.7%, my calculation).
opinion: |-
  SUBLIME shows that LIRA is feasible in 6 centers, in elderly patients, nearly all Sievers 1, with self-expanding supra-annular valves.

  It does not show an advantage over annular sizing, "confirmed" safety, absence of bias between measurements, or consistency across centers. There are no data in Sievers 0 and almost none in Sievers 2.

  What I would ask the authors for: the same outcomes in patients sized by the annulus over the same period.
