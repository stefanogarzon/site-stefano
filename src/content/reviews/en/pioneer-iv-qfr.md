summary: |
  In the interim analysis, QFR-guided PCI was declared non-inferior to usual care. The patient-oriented composite endpoint (POCE) occurred in 6.9% with QFR and 6.8% with usual care (non-inferiority p 0.015).

  In the usual care arm, 41% of patients had physiological assessment (FFR or an angiography-derived index) and 59% had none.
strengths: |
  - Randomized, multicenter, in an all-comer population.
problems: |
  - **Non-inferiority hypothesis for a tool meant to improve decisions.** Non-inferiority makes sense when QFR replaces FFR: same outcome, no pressure wire. That is what FAVOR III Europe tested. In PIONEER IV, 59% of usual care used no physiology. For those patients, QFR is an addition, and the hypothesis should be superiority.
  - **Heterogeneous comparator.** 41% of the usual care arm used FFR or angiography-derived physiology, and 59% used none. It is unclear against what QFR was shown to be non-inferior.
opinion: |
  "Not worse" is not a reason to adopt. If QFR adds no benefit over a usual care that used no physiology in 59% of cases, there is no reason to adopt it. The question raised by FAVOR III Europe remains open.
